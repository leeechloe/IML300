Vibe Coding Projects

Vibe Coding #1: Flower Bouquet

I wanted to create a website where you can build a custom flower bouquet by dragging flowers into a vase and virtually send it to someone with a heartfelt.

Vibe Coding #2: Digital Companion

Inspired by Frank Chimero's What Screen Wants, this vibe coding experiment explores screens as a living, responsive material. I asked Claude to build an emotional interface on relationship than task. A blob reacts to the cursor's behavior like gentle hovers build trust and repeated clicks create jittery anxiety.

Vibe Coding #3: The Little House

I read Laurel Schwulst’s What Is a Website? and wanted to create a personal website as a dollhouse, where each room holds fragments of thoughts, experiences, and ideas. Visitors are invited to explore freely, emphasizing curiosity and process. The dollhouse functions as a lived-in space rather than a platform.

Vibe Coding #4: What's in My Bag

I enjoy Molly Soda's aesthetics and her authenticity when it comes to her work. I was inspired by her work "What's in My Bag?" and I wanted to create my own. This interactive piece captures that same intimate, mundane energy like the random, unglamorous stuff that actually lives in your bag, pulled out one by one.

Vibe Coding #5: Little Things

Inspired by chia amisola's when we love and her simplistic, soft, and dreamy aesthetic, little things is a love letter to the ordinary. it's a gentle reminder to slow down and appreciate what we often overlook because the little things in life are the most beautiful things of all.

Vibe Coding #6: Daily Habit Tracker

During midterm season, it's easy to forget to take care of yourself physically and mentally. Inspired by Brandon Gregory's focus on creating user experiences that aren't overwhelming, this is a calm, simple habit tracker designed to gently remind you to take care of yourself.

Vibe Coding #7: Algorithm Says No

I wanted to vibe code a playful web interaction where users enter a description of themselves, and the algorithm produces absurd answers instead of data-driven labels. Inspired by Mindy Seu's Cyberfeminism Index, the vibe code resists algorithmic categorization by replacing data logic with humor and unpredictability, as if it has a mind of its own.

Vibe Coding #8: Keyboard Synesthesia

Inspired by Olivia Jack's projects, where she builds live coding and performance. I wanted to create something similar: when you press key on your keyboard, it makes music. Each key has a beat and corresponds to a color, so you create music through sound and color. I had a lot of making this one because of how interactive it was.
